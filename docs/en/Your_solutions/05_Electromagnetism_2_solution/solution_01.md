# Task 01 – Gauss's Law

## Problem Statement

A point charge of $+2 \ \mathrm{C}$ is located at the origin. Calculate the electric flux through a spherical surface of radius $1 \ \mathrm{m}$ centered at the origin.

## Theory

Gauss's law states that the total electric flux through any closed surface is equal to the enclosed charge divided by the permittivity of free space:

$$
\Phi_E = \frac{Q_{\mathrm{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}
$$

where:

- $\Phi_E$ is the electric flux,
- $Q_{\mathrm{enc}}$ is the charge enclosed by the surface,
- $\varepsilon_0 = 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \ \mathrm{C^2/(N \cdot m^2)}$.

For a closed spherical surface centered on a point charge, the radius does not affect the total flux. The flux depends only on the enclosed charge.

## Step-by-Step Solution

The enclosed charge is

$$
Q_{\mathrm{enc}} = 2 \ \mathrm{C}
$$

Using Gauss's law,

$$
\Phi_E = \frac{Q_{\mathrm{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}
$$

Substitute the known values:

$$
\Phi_E = \frac{2}{8.854 \times 10^{-12}}
$$

Calculate:

$$
\Phi_E \approx 2.26 \times 10^{11} \ \mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}
$$

## Final Result

$$
\Phi_E \approx 2.26 \times 10^{11} \ \mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}
$$

## Interpretation

The electric flux is positive because the enclosed charge is positive. Electric field lines leave a positive charge, so the net flux through the spherical surface is outward.
