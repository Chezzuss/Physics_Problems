# Task 02 – Ampere's Law

## Problem Statement

Two long, parallel wires are $10 \ \mathrm{cm}$ apart and carry currents of $5 \ \mathrm{A}$ in opposite directions. Calculate the magnitude and direction of the magnetic field at a point midway between the wires.

## Theory

The magnetic field produced by a long straight current-carrying wire at distance $r$ is

$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2 \pi r}
$$

where:

- $B$ is the magnetic field,
- $\mu_0 = 4 \pi \times 10^{-7} \ \mathrm{T \cdot m/A}$,
- $I$ is the current,
- $r$ is the distance from the wire.

The direction of the magnetic field is determined using the right-hand rule.

## Step-by-Step Solution

The distance between the wires is

$$
d = 10 \ \mathrm{cm} = 0.10 \ \mathrm{m}
$$

The midpoint is halfway between the wires, so the distance from each wire is

$$
r = \frac{d}{2} = \frac{0.10}{2} = 0.05 \ \mathrm{m}
$$

The magnetic field due to one wire is

$$
B_1 = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2 \pi r}
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
B_1 = \frac{(4 \pi \times 10^{-7})(5)}{2 \pi (0.05)}
$$

Simplify:

$$
B_1 = 2.0 \times 10^{-5} \ \mathrm{T}
$$

$$

$$
B_{\mathrm{total}} = 2B_1

$$

## Final Result

$$
B_{\mathrm{total}} = 4.0 \times 10^{-5} \ \mathrm{T}
$$

## Interpretation

The magnetic fields from the two wires add at the midpoint because the currents are in opposite directions. The exact direction depends on which wire carries current into or out of the page. Using the right-hand rule, the field at the midpoint is perpendicular to the line joining the wires.
