# Task 04 – Magnetic Torque

## Problem Statement

A rectangular loop of wire with dimensions $10 \ \mathrm{cm}$ by $5 \ \mathrm{cm}$ carries a current of $2 \ \mathrm{A}$. A uniform magnetic field of $B = 0.3 \ \mathrm{T}$ is applied parallel to the plane of the loop. What is the magnitude of the magnetic torque on the loop?

## Theory

The torque on a current loop in a uniform magnetic field is

$$
\tau = NIAB \sin \theta
$$

where:

- $\tau$ is the magnetic torque,
- $N$ is the number of turns,
- $I$ is the current,
- $A$ is the area of the loop,
- $B$ is the magnetic field strength,
- $\theta$ is the angle between the magnetic field and the normal vector to the plane of the loop.

If the magnetic field is parallel to the plane of the loop, then it is perpendicular to the loop's normal vector. Therefore,

$$
\theta = 90^\circ
$$

and

$$
\sin \theta = 1
$$

Assume the loop has one turn:

$$
N = 1
$$

## Step-by-Step Solution

Convert the loop dimensions to meters:

$$
10 \ \mathrm{cm} = 0.10 \ \mathrm{m}
$$

$$
5 \ \mathrm{cm} = 0.05 \ \mathrm{m}
$$

The area of the rectangular loop is

$$
A = lw
$$

$$
A = (0.10)(0.05)
$$

$$
A = 0.005 \ \mathrm{m^2}
$$

The torque is

$$
\tau = NIAB \sin \theta
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
\tau = (1)(2)(0.005)(0.3)(1)
$$

Calculate:

$$
\tau = 0.003 \ \mathrm{N \cdot m}
$$

## Final Result

$$
\tau = 3.0 \times 10^{-3} \ \mathrm{N \cdot m}
$$

## Interpretation

The torque is maximum because the magnetic field is parallel to the plane of the loop. In this position, the angle between the magnetic field and the loop's area vector is $90^\circ$.
