# Task 05 – Energy Stored by Charge in a Capacitor

## Problem Statement

A parallel-plate capacitor has the following parameters:

- Plate area: $S = 0.02 \ \mathrm{m^2}$
- Plate separation: $d = 5 \ \mathrm{mm}$
- Voltage: $V = 500 \ \mathrm{V}$

Calculate:

1. The capacitance $C$ of the capacitor.
2. The energy stored in the capacitor.
3. The electric field intensity $E$ between the plates.
4. The force of attraction $F$ between the plates.

## Theory

For a parallel-plate capacitor in air or vacuum, the capacitance is

$$
C = \varepsilon_0 \frac{S}{d}
$$

where:

- $C$ is capacitance,
- $\varepsilon_0 = 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \ \mathrm{F/m}$,
- $S$ is the plate area,
- $d$ is the plate separation.

The energy stored in a capacitor is

$$
W = \frac{1}{2}CV^2
$$

The electric field between parallel plates is

$$
E = \frac{V}{d}
$$

The attractive force between capacitor plates is

$$
F = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2 S
$$

## Step-by-Step Solution

The plate separation is

$$
d = 5 \ \mathrm{mm} = 5 \times 10^{-3} \ \mathrm{m}
$$

### Capacitance

Using

$$
C = \varepsilon_0 \frac{S}{d}
$$

substitute the values:

$$
C = (8.854 \times 10^{-12}) \frac{0.02}{5 \times 10^{-3}}
$$

Calculate the ratio:

$$
\frac{0.02}{5 \times 10^{-3}} = 4
$$

Therefore,

$$
C = (8.854 \times 10^{-12})(4)
$$

$$
C = 3.54 \times 10^{-11} \ \mathrm{F}
$$

### Stored Energy

The stored energy is

$$
W = \frac{1}{2}CV^2
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
W = \frac{1}{2}(3.54 \times 10^{-11})(500)^2
$$

Calculate:

$$
W = \frac{1}{2}(3.54 \times 10^{-11})(250000)
$$

$$
W = 4.43 \times 10^{-6} \ \mathrm{J}
$$

### Electric Field

The electric field is

$$
E = \frac{V}{d}
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
E = \frac{500}{5 \times 10^{-3}}
$$

$$
E = 1.0 \times 10^5 \ \mathrm{V/m}
$$

### Force Between Plates

The attractive force is

$$
F = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2 S
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
F = \frac{1}{2}(8.854 \times 10^{-12})(1.0 \times 10^5)^2(0.02)
$$

Calculate the square:

$$
(1.0 \times 10^5)^2 = 1.0 \times 10^{10}
$$

Then

$$
F = \frac{1}{2}(8.854 \times 10^{-12})(1.0 \times 10^{10})(0.02)
$$

$$
F = 8.85 \times 10^{-4} \ \mathrm{N}
$$

## Final Result

$$
C = 3.54 \times 10^{-11} \ \mathrm{F}
$$

$$
W = 4.43 \times 10^{-6} \ \mathrm{J}
$$

$$
E = 1.0 \times 10^5 \ \mathrm{V/m}
$$

$$
F = 8.85 \times 10^{-4} \ \mathrm{N}
$$

## Interpretation

The capacitance is small because the plate separation is several millimeters and the plate area is modest. The electric field is large because a voltage of $500 \ \mathrm{V}$ is applied across only $5 \ \mathrm{mm}$. The attractive force between the plates is produced by the electric field energy stored between them.
