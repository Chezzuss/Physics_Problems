# Task 10 – Average Current

## Problem Statement

A lightning bolt transfers a charge of $30 \ \mathrm{C}$ to the ground in a time of $2 \ \mathrm{ms}$.

Find the average current of the lightning bolt.

## Theory

Electric current is defined as the rate of flow of electric charge:

$$
I = \frac{Q}{t}
$$

where:

- $I$ is the current,
- $Q$ is the transferred charge,
- $t$ is the time interval.

The SI unit of current is the ampere:

$$
1 \ \mathrm{A} = 1 \ \mathrm{C/s}
$$

## Step-by-Step Solution

The transferred charge is

$$
Q = 30 \ \mathrm{C}
$$

The time interval is

$$
t = 2 \ \mathrm{ms}
$$

Convert milliseconds to seconds:

$$
2 \ \mathrm{ms} = 2 \times 10^{-3} \ \mathrm{s}
$$

Use the current formula:

$$
I = \frac{Q}{t}
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
I = \frac{30}{2 \times 10^{-3}}
$$

$$
I = 1.5 \times 10^4 \ \mathrm{A}
$$

## Final Result

$$
I = 1.5 \times 10^4 \ \mathrm{A}
$$

or

$$
I = 15000 \ \mathrm{A}
$$

## Interpretation

Lightning carries an extremely large electric current because a large amount of charge is transferred in a very short time interval.
