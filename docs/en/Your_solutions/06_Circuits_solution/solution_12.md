# Task 12 – Transformer Currents

## Problem Statement

A transformer has:

- primary coil turns: $N_p = 1000$
- secondary coil turns: $N_s = 200$
- primary voltage: $V_p = 120 \ \mathrm{V}$

Find:

1. The secondary voltage
2. The primary current if the secondary current is

$$
I_s = 3 \ \mathrm{A}
$$

## Theory

An ideal transformer transfers electrical energy between two coils through electromagnetic induction.

The voltage ratio in a transformer is proportional to the turns ratio:

$$
\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}
$$

where:

- $V_p$ is the primary voltage,
- $V_s$ is the secondary voltage,
- $N_p$ is the number of turns in the primary coil,
- $N_s$ is the number of turns in the secondary coil.

The current ratio is inversely proportional to the turns ratio:

$$
\frac{I_s}{I_p} = \frac{N_p}{N_s}
$$

or equivalently,

$$
I_p = I_s \frac{N_s}{N_p}
$$

For an ideal transformer, power is conserved:

$$
P_p = P_s
$$

Therefore,

$$
V_p I_p = V_s I_s
$$

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Secondary Voltage

Use the transformer voltage equation:

$$
\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}
$$

Substitute the known values:

$$
\frac{V_s}{120} = \frac{200}{1000}
$$

Simplify the fraction:

$$
\frac{200}{1000} = 0.2
$$

Thus,

$$
\frac{V_s}{120} = 0.2
$$

Multiply both sides by $120$:

$$
V_s = 120 \times 0.2
$$

Therefore,

$$
V_s = 24 \ \mathrm{V}
$$

### Primary Current

Use the transformer current relation:

$$
I_p = I_s \frac{N_s}{N_p}
$$

Substitute the known values:

$$
I_p = 3 \times \frac{200}{1000}
$$

Simplify the fraction:

$$
\frac{200}{1000} = 0.2
$$

Thus,

$$
I_p = 3 \times 0.2
$$

Therefore,

$$
I_p = 0.6 \ \mathrm{A}
$$

## Final Result

The secondary voltage is

$$
V_s = 24 \ \mathrm{V}
$$

The primary current is

$$
I_p = 0.6 \ \mathrm{A}
$$

## Interpretation

The transformer reduces the voltage from $120 \ \mathrm{V}$ to $24 \ \mathrm{V}$ because the secondary coil has fewer turns than the primary coil.

This is a step-down transformer.

As the voltage decreases, the current increases on the secondary side in order to conserve power. The primary current is smaller because the primary voltage is larger.
